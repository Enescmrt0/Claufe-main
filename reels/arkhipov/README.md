## Facts

During the Cuban Missile Crisis, Soviet submarine B-59 carried a nuclear-tipped torpedo. Cut off from Moscow, with failing batteries and
air conditioning and heat above 45 °C, it was harassed by US destroyers dropping signalling depth charges on 27 October 1962. Captain
Valentin Savitsky, believing war had begun, ordered the torpedo prepared ("We're going to die, but we will sink them all"). Launch required
the agreement of the captain, political officer Ivan Maslennikov and flotilla chief of staff Vasili Arkhipov; Arkhipov refused and talked
the captain down. The submarine surfaced among US ships and returned home, where the crew got a cold reception (one officer reportedly said
it would have been better had they gone down with their ship). The story became widely known at the 2002 Havana conference, after which
Thomas Blanton of the National Security Archive said "a guy called Vasili Arkhipov saved the world". Arkhipov died in 1998.
