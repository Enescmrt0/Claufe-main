## Facts

- Juan Pujol García, a Spaniard who opposed the Nazis, offered to spy for Britain and was turned down several times.
- He got himself recruited by the Abwehr by claiming he was going to London. Instead he worked from Lisbon and invented
  his reports from a guidebook, maps, reference books and newsreels. The reports contained errors, for example that
  Glaswegians "would do anything for a litre of wine".
- British codebreakers saw the Germans acting on his invented reports, including hunting a convoy that did not exist.
  MI5 brought him to London in 1942 under the codename Garbo.
- His network grew to 27 fictitious agents, whose pay and expenses Germany funded.
- He sent a warning about Normandy hours before D-Day. It was timed to arrive too late to matter. On 9 June he reported
  that Normandy was a diversion and that the main attack would come at Pas-de-Calais.
- Hitler kept forces waiting there for weeks.
- He received the Iron Cross (July 1944) and the MBE (December 1944).
