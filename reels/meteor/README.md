# Ann Hodges: hit by a meteorite on her couch (Sylacauga, Alabama, 30 November 1954)

Stick-figure reel, old (v1) engine, am_michael voice at 1.12, about 51 s. Props are in `web/env63.js`: Ann with curls and bruise, landlady, airman, 1950s house exterior with roof hole, living room with ceiling hole and dust, couch, console radio, meteorite with glow, fireball, town street, grapefruit and pineapple comparisons, $500 stack, museum case.

**Facts used**
- On 30 November 1954 a meteorite of about 3.9 kg (8.5 lb), roughly grapefruit-sized, crashed through the roof of Ann Hodges's rented house in Sylacauga, Alabama, while she napped on the couch. A fireball was seen across the area.
- It bounced off a large console radio and struck her hip, leaving a large bruise (widely described as pineapple-sized). She is the first well-documented case of a person struck by a meteorite.
- The US Air Force took the meteorite for examination. Her landlady, Birdie Guy, claimed it as her property. After a legal dispute, Hodges paid her $500 to settle and keep it.
- By then public interest had faded and no buyer was found. In 1956 Hodges donated it to the Alabama Museum of Natural History (University of Alabama), where it is on display.
- Two lines were reworded before recording (no "lunchtime" time claim, no claim about the roof hole being displayed) to avoid unverified details.
