# The London Beer Flood (17 October 1814)

Stick-figure reel, old (v1) engine, am_michael voice at 1.12, about 41 s. Props are in `web/env56.js`.

**Facts used**
- At Meux's Horse Shoe Brewery (Tottenham Court Road), a 22 ft wooden vat held about 610,000 litres of porter and was bound by iron hoops.
- On 17 October 1814 one hoop slipped. About an hour later the vat burst, rupturing other vats; roughly 1.47 million litres of beer broke through the brewery's back wall.
- A wave reported up to 15 ft (4.6 m) high flooded the slum of St Giles, filling basements. Eight people died.
- Some people collected the beer in pots and pans. Later stories of mass drunkenness are not supported by contemporary reports.
- An inquest ruled it an act of God ("casually, accidentally and by misfortune"), so nobody was blamed.
- The brewery was refunded the excise duty it had paid on the lost beer (about £7,250).
