## Facts

- The Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition aimed to make the first land crossing of Antarctica. Endurance left Grytviken, South Georgia, on 5 December 1914 with 28 men (including the stowaway Perce Blackborow), 69 dogs and photographer Frank Hurley.
- In January 1915 she was beset in the Weddell Sea, about a day's sail from the planned landing at Vahsel Bay. Thomas Orde-Lees wrote she was frozen in "like an almond in the middle of a chocolate bar".
- She drifted with the pack for about nine months. The men played football on the ice and trained dogs through the polar night.
- Pressure crushed the ship. Shackleton ordered "abandon ship" on 27 October 1915, and she sank on 21 November 1915.
- The men camped on the floes (Ocean Camp, Patience Camp), ate seals and penguins, and eventually the dogs. When the floe broke up in April 1916 they took to the three boats and reached Elephant Island after about seven days: the first land in 497 days.
- Elephant Island was far from shipping lanes. Shackleton and five men sailed the 6.9 m James Caird about 1,300 km (800 nautical miles) to South Georgia in about 16 days. Frank Worsley navigated with only four sun sightings.
- They landed at King Haakon Bay on the uninhabited south side. Shackleton, Worsley and Tom Crean crossed the unmapped interior in about 36 hours, with screws in their boots, glissaded down a slope on a coiled rope, heard the 7 am whistle at Stromness, and arrived unrecognised by the station manager, Thoralf Sørlle.
- Three rescue attempts were blocked by ice. The fourth, on the Chilean tug Yelcho (pilot Luis Pardo), reached Elephant Island on 30 August 1916. All 22 men there were alive, so all 28 of the Endurance party survived. The Ross Sea support party, on the other side of the continent, is not part of this story.
- The wreck was found in March 2022 at about 3,008 m depth, remarkably intact, with ENDURANCE still legible on the stern.
