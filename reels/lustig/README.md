## Facts

In 1925 Victor Lustig, posing with forged papers as a government official, told scrap-metal dealers at the Hôtel de
Crillon that the city would sell the Eiffel Tower for scrap. André Poisson grew suspicious; Lustig "confessed" to
wanting a bribe, which convinced him. Lustig fled to Austria with the money; Poisson, embarrassed, did not report it.
Lustig returned and tried again; a dealer went to the police and Lustig escaped. He was later convicted in the US for
counterfeiting and sent to Alcatraz.
