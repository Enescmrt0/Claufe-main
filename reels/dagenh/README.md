## Facts

Sweden drove on the left although its neighbours drove on the right and most Swedish cars were left-hand drive. In a 1955 referendum
82.9% voted against switching, but parliament approved it in 1963. Dagen H ("H" for höger, right) was Sunday 3 September 1967: non-essential
traffic was banned overnight, vehicles stopped at 04:50, carefully changed sides and restarted at 05:00. About 360,000 signs were changed
(new ones pre-installed and covered); a contest produced the song "Håll dig till höger, Svensson". Accidents fell in the period right
after the switch as drivers were extra careful.
