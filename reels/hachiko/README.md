# Hachiko: the dog who waited almost ten years (Tokyo, 1924-1935)

Stick-figure reel, old (v1) engine, am_michael voice at 1.12, about 60 s. Props are in `web/env62.js`: an Akita (standing, walking, sitting, bronze), Professor Ueno, Shibuya station in six moods (day, evening, night, rain, snow, spring), a 1920s train, a calendar flip, a newspaper, the statue and a modern Tokyo skyline.

**Facts used**
- In 1924, Hidesaburō Ueno, an agriculture professor at Tokyo Imperial University, took in an Akita puppy, Hachikō. The dog walked with him to Shibuya Station and met him there in the evening.
- On 21 May 1925, Ueno died suddenly of a cerebral haemorrhage at work. Hachikō kept returning to the station every day at train time.
- Some station staff shooed him away; others fed him. A 1932 article in the Tokyo Asahi Shimbun made him nationally famous as a symbol of loyalty.
- A bronze statue was unveiled at Shibuya Station in 1934 with Hachikō present.
- He died on 8 March 1935, about nine years and ten months after Ueno's death. The original statue was melted down in WWII and recast in 1948. The Hachikō statue is today one of Tokyo's most famous meeting spots.
