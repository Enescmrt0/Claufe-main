## Facts

After the Treaties of Tilsit (July 1807), Napoleon's chief of staff Louis-Alexandre Berthier arranged a rabbit hunt; accounts
give hundreds to as many as 3,000 rabbits. When released, the rabbits rushed toward Napoleon and his party instead of fleeing;
whips and sticks failed, Napoleon retreated to his carriage, and the rabbits are said to have flanked it and jumped inside. The
usual explanation is that Berthier had bought tame rabbits from farmers, which took the people for their feeders. The story
comes from the memoirs of General Paul Thiébault and is not independently confirmed.
