## Facts

In late 1932, after WWI veterans had been settled on wheat farms in Western Australia, around 20,000 emus moved into
the farmland. The Defence Minister sent Major G. P. W. Meredith with two soldiers, two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds.
The emus scattered, a gun jammed during an ambush at a dam, and a truck-mounted gun could not keep up or aim on the
rough ground; the army withdrew after about a week. A second attempt claimed 986 kills with 9,860 rounds (about 10 per
bird). Meredith compared the birds' "bullet-carrying capacity" to an army that could face anyone. Bounty schemes and
exclusion fences later worked better; emus are still common across Australia.
