## Facts

- Held during the 1904 World's Fair in St. Louis on 30 August, in heat around 32 °C on dusty dirt roads with hills; 32 started, 14 finished.
- Officials' cars drove ahead and raised dust. There was only one water source (a well around mile 12), because organizer James Sullivan
  wanted to study "purposeful dehydration".
- William Garcia collapsed with internal bleeding from inhaled dust and nearly died; another runner quit after vomiting.
- Cuban mailman Andarín Carvajal lost his travel money gambling in New Orleans, hitchhiked to St. Louis, ran in cut-off trousers, ate rotten
  apples, napped by the road and still finished fourth.
- Len Tau and Jan Mashiani (Tswana) were the first black Africans to compete in the Olympics; Tau was chased about a mile off course by
  wild dogs and finished ninth.
- Fred Lorz quit with cramps after nine miles, rode about eleven miles in a car, ran the rest and crossed first; he was photographed with
  Alice Roosevelt before being exposed, claimed it was a joke and was banned for life (later lifted; he won the 1905 Boston Marathon).
- Thomas Hicks, English-born and running for the US, was given strychnine with egg whites, then a second dose with brandy; he hallucinated,
  was half-carried over the line, won in 3:28:53 (still the slowest winning Olympic marathon time) and lost about eight pounds.
- Officials considered dropping the marathon; the 1908 London Olympics marathon had refreshment stations.
